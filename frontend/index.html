The basics

What are carbon emissions?

Carbon emissions is a common shorthand for greenhouse gases released by human activity. Carbon dioxide (CO₂) is one; methane (CH₄) is another. These gases trap heat in the atmosphere, contributing to global warming. Because different gases have different warming effects, emissions are often expressed as carbon dioxide equivalent (CO₂e), a shared unit for comparing them. A waste item's estimated impact depends on its material and weight, along with the lifecycle stage and disposal pathway included in the calculation.

Why it matters

A changing climate affects people and nature.

Greenhouse-gas emissions contribute to long-term warming, with consequences that vary across regions and communities.

Rising temperatures

Long-term warming increases the likelihood and intensity of extreme heat in many parts of the world.

Weather extremes

Climate change can intensify some heavy rainfall, drought, and other extremes, while local outcomes differ.

Rising seas

Warming oceans and land-ice loss contribute to sea-level rise, increasing risks for many coastal areas.

Nature and health

Changes in heat, water, and habitats can place added pressure on ecosystems, food systems, and human health.

Why waste matters

Materials have different stories — and different impacts.

Emissions can occur when materials are extracted, manufactured, transported, and treated after use. Food and other organic waste can release methane when it decomposes in landfill conditions. Plastics, paper, metals, wood, and organics each have different production and disposal pathways, so their estimated footprints are not interchangeable.
